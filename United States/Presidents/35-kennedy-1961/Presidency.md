# Quick Facts
- Full Name: John F. Kennedy
- Party: Democrat
# Personal Life:
- Born: 1917 in Massachusetts
- Catholic family
- Came from Boston Brahman elite family:
	- Father (Joe Kennedy) was a multi-millionaire in banking, bootlegging, shipbuilding, and the film industry. Also was head of SEC and US ambassador to Great Britain
	- Mother was daughter of John F. (“Honey Fitz”) Fitzgerald, onetime mayor of Boston
- Suffered from Addison disease (rare adrenal gland disorder)
- Harvard for undergraduate
- Joined US Navy in 1941 and saw action in the Solomon Islands
	- Awarded the U.S. Navy and Marine Corps Medal for heroism
	- In war got back injury and despite operations in 1944, 1954, and 1955, he was in pain for much of the rest of his life.
		- Eventually began taking injections of amphetamines to cope with back pain and Addison disease, reported he used injections weekly during his presidency and became dependent
- Switched from pursuing an academic or journalistic career when his brother Joe Kennedy died in the war in 1945 (Joe was expected to be the first Kennedy to run for office)
- Married Jacqueline Kennedy in 1953: came from socially prominent family and was the perfect complement to the handsome politician
- Writing Career:
	- *Why England Slept* (1940)
	- *Profiles in Courage* (1956) -> Pulitzer Prize winner (his assistant Theodore Sorensen did much of the research and writing)
# Political life

- Served as his secretary for his father when he was US ambassador to Britain in 1938
- Never lost and election:

- Ran for Congress as a Democrat in 1946 (campaigned aggressively) at age 29 and won by landslide to win 11th congressional district US House seat.
- In 1952, he ran for the U.S. Senate against the popular incumbent, Henry Cabot Lodge, Jr. and won by 70,000 votes despite Republican Eisenhower winning presidency in the state.
- In 1958, he ran for the Senate and won by the largest ever in Massachusetts politics -> his evidence he was popular enough to go for the presidency

- US House Rep from 1947–53

- bread-and-butter liberal
- advocated better working conditions, more public housing, higher wages, lower prices, cheaper rents, more Social Security for elderly
- backed Cold War policies like Truman Doctrine and the Marshall Plan but critical of Truman Asia policy (like Chiang Kai-shek in China)

- US Senator from 1953-61

- 1954 he was the only New England senator to approve an extension of President Eisenhower’s reciprocal-trade powers
- backed the opening of the St. Lawrence Seaway despite unpopularity in Massachusetts
- Downplayed excesses of McCarthyism (Kennedy's father was a McCarthy funder and backer) and was absent on the day of the vote to censure McCarthy’s conduct despite himself disapproved of McCarthy as he was in the hospital for his back pain
- Moved leftward politically: led fight against a proposal to abolish the electoral college, crusaded for labor reform, and became increasingly committed to civil rights legislation
- Member of the Senate Committee on Foreign Relations: advocated extensive foreign aid to the emerging nations in Africa and Asia, and he surprisingly called upon France to grant Algerian independence.

- Presidential Candidacy

- Was nearly the Democrat VP candidate in 1956 -> helped him become one of the best-known political figures in the country
- Formally announced in January 1960

- Opponents were senators Hubert H. Humphrey of Minnesota and Lyndon B. Johnson of Texas.

- Campaigned very aggressively b/c of the widespread conviction that no Roman Catholic candidate could be elected president.

- Kennedy won in West Virginia primary, a heavily Protestant state, proving that he could win in a state with few Catholics
- Tackled the Catholic issue by avowing his belief in the separation of church and state in a televised speech before a group of Protestant ministers in Houston
- Kennedy’s carefully calculated pursuit of the presidency years before the first primary established a practice that became the norm for future candidates
- Had massive campaign support from his brothers RFK and Ted Kennedy. Both were Harvard and UVA Law grads.
- Kennedy's and Nixon's presidential campaigns were the longest and the most intensive ever held in the United States

- Won DNC in July 1960 on first ballot and surprisingly chose Johnson as his running mate to balance the ticket.

- LBJ was a southern Democrat so we would gain support from southern states skeptical of JFK's Catholicism and since traditional southern Democratic leanings were changing.

- New Frontier:

- [Acceptance speech](https://www.jfklibrary.org/learn/about-jfk/historic-speeches/acceptance-of-democratic-nomination-for-president): "We stand today on the edge of a New Frontier"
- Described his concept of the challenges facing the US in the 1960s
- Platform:

- Expand the country’s defense and foreign aid programs
- Controversially, also committed to protecting African American civil rights

- "Kennedy style": glamorous, elitist, and charismatic. Jacqueline Kennedy’s beauty and fashion sense, the charm of their children and relatives, and the Harvard advisers who surrounded him.

- Elections

- November 8, 1960:

- Nixon:

- Never faced any serious opposition in Republican primaries

- VP Pick:  Henry Cabot Lodge, Jr., a former U.S. senator from Massachusetts

- Defended Eisenhower's popular record (was his VP) but said he would improve welfare programs, foreign aid, and defense.
- Major factor in the campaign was a unique series of four televised debates between the two men; an estimated 85–120 million Americans watched one or more of the debates.

- Many viewed Kennedy as the winner in the debates despite Nixon's mastery of the issues due to his poise in front of the camera, Harvard accent, and his good looks (in contrast to Nixon’s "five o’clock shadow")

- Harry F. Byrd:

- prominent Democratic politician in the state of Virginia for more than 40 years
- Elected governor of the state in 1925 and appointed U.S. senator in 1933
- Coauthored the "Southern Manifesto" (1956), a document signed by numerous Southern politicians calling for "massive resistance" to in Brown v. Board of Education.
- Embraced low taxes, minimal government services (anti-New Deal), administrative efficiency, and white supremacy.

- Results:

- Electors:

- Kennedy (303)
- Nixon (219)
- Byrd (15): not a candidate but won 6 unpledged electors in Alabama, 8 unpledged electors in Mississippi and 1 pledged elector in Oklahoma.

- Beat Nixon by a margin of less than 120,000 out of some 70,000,000 votes cast
- Widely believed that voter fraud in Illinois and Texas contributed to Kennedy’s victory, especially in Illinois where Joe Kennedy enlisted the help of Richard J. Daley (mayor of Chicago)

- Despite Eisenhower urging Nixon to contest the results, he didn’t want to set a bad example for newly democratic nations abroad. Nixon was widely praised for his dignity in his defeat.

- Kennedy was the first Catholic and the youngest person ever elected president
