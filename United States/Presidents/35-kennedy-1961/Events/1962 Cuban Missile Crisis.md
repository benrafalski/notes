# Overview
- October 1962: buildup of Soviet nuclear missiles was discovered in Cuba
- Kennedy demanded missiles be dismantled and ordered a "quarantine” of Cuba (effective blockade)
- For 13 days nuclear war seemed near
- Ended when Khrushchev withdrew the missiles
# Background
- May 1960: Khrushchev promised to defend Cuba with Soviet arms, assuming the US would take no steps to prevent the installation of Soviet medium- and intermediate-range ballistic missiles on the island
	- The missiles could hit much of the eastern US within a few minutes if launched
- July 1962: Soviets begin missile shipments to Cuba
- August 29, 1962: US U-2 spy planes flying over Cuba report new military construction and the presence of Soviet technicians
- October 14, 1962: US reports presence of a ballistic missile on a launching site in Cuba
# Crisis
- Kennedy considered alternatives to an immediate U.S. invasion/strikes on Cuba)
- October 22, 1962: instead his admin place a naval "quarantine" (blockade) on Cuba to prevent further Soviet shipments of missiles.
	- Said U.S. forces would seize "offensive weapons and associated matériel" that Soviet vessels might attempt to deliver to Cuba
	- Soviet ships bound for Cuba altered course away from the quarantined zone as a result
- Kennedy and Khrushchev exchanged messages over following days as they headed to nuclear war
- October 28, 1962: Khrushchev capitulates, halting missile site construction and returning the Cuban missiles to the Soviet Union
	- Kennedy committed the United States to never invading Cuba in return and also secretly promised to withdraw the US nuclear-armed missiles stationed in Turkey in previous years.
- Crisis fully ended by late November 1962 as their promises were fulfilled
# Aftermath
- Castro was big mad at the Soviets for retreating
- Retreat humiliated Khrushchev and is believed to have greatly contributed to his ousting as premier in 1964
