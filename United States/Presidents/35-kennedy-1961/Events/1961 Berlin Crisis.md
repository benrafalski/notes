# Overview
- Khrushchev ordered a wall built between East and West Berlin in 1961
- Wall came to symbolize the Cold War’s division of East from West Germany and of eastern from western Europe

# Background
- Between 1949-1961, about 2.5 million East Germans had fled from East to West Germany (massive brain drain) threatening to destroy the economic viability of East Germany
- Soviet premier, Nikita Khrushchev, thought Kennedy was too young after the two leaders met in Vienna in June 1961 and that he could bully him
- Khrushchev escalates Cold War tensions:
	- Demands Western forces withdraw from West Berlin
	- Renews threats to sign a separate peace treaty with East Germany
	- Orders the building of the Wall after Kennedy refuses to back down

# The Wall
- First erected on the night of August 12–13, 1961, as the result of a decree passed on August 12 by the East German Volkskammer (“Peoples’ Chamber”)
- Original wall was built of barbed wire and cinder blocks but replaced by concrete walls topped with barbed wire and guarded with watchtowers + mines.
- To counter Soviet aggression and signal resolve, Kennedy activated National Guard and reserve units in summer 1961.
	- Caused Khrushchev to back down from his separate peace treaty threat
- Kennedy then made a dramatic visit to West Berlin saying, "Today, in the world of freedom, the proudest boast is 'Ich bin ein \[I am a\] Berliner.'"
