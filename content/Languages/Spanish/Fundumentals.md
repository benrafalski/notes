# Spanish A1: 30-Day Study Guide

**Goal:** Build the skills described at CEFR A1: understand and use familiar everyday expressions, introduce yourself, ask and answer simple questions, and manage short exchanges when the other person speaks clearly and helps you.

**Time:** Aim for 75 minutes a day, six or seven days a week. Thirty days can build a solid A1 foundation, but reaching A1 depends on your starting point, practice quality, and how much real listening and speaking you do. This is a study plan, not a certification guarantee.

## Daily routine

Use this same routine every day. On Day 1, use the review block to revisit the sounds and phrases you already know.

- [ ] **10 min | Recall:** Without notes, say or write yesterday's words and sentences. Check, correct, and repeat the missed items.
- [ ] **15 min | Learn:** Study today's small grammar point and 8-12 useful words or phrases.
- [ ] **15 min | Listen:** Use beginner Spanish audio or video. Listen once for the general idea, then again and repeat short phrases aloud. Try [Dreaming Spanish beginner videos](https://www.dreamingspanish.com/browse?level=superbeginner) or the [Language Transfer Spanish course](https://www.languagetransfer.org/complete-spanish).
- [ ] **15 min | Speak:** Answer today's prompt aloud without reading. Record yourself when possible; listen back and correct one thing.
- [ ] **15 min | Read and write:** Read a short beginner text or today's examples, then write 3-6 original sentences.
- [ ] **5 min | Exit check:** Put notes away and complete today's check from memory.

Keep a small flashcard deck for words you actually need. Review cards daily; make cards with a Spanish word or phrase on the front and meaning plus one example on the back. Learn nouns with their article (*la casa*, not just *casa*).

## Quick reference

### Pronunciation

Spanish spelling is fairly consistent. Listen and imitate rather than relying on English approximations.

- **a, e, i, o, u** have steady vowel sounds; **h** is silent.
- **j** and **g** before *e/i* sound like a strong breathy *h* in much of Spain and Latin America.
- **ñ** is like the *ny* in “canyon”; **ll** and **y** vary by region.
- **r** is tapped; **rr** is trilled. Don't let perfecting the trill hold you back.
- Written accents mark stress or distinguish words: *sí* = yes, *si* = if; *¿Qué?* = What?

### Essential phrases

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| Hola. / Buenos días. | Hello. / Good morning. |
| ¿Qué tal? / ¿Cómo estás? | How's it going? / How are you? |
| Estoy bien, gracias. ¿Y tú? | I'm well, thanks. And you? |
| Me llamo ___. / Soy ___. | My name is ___. / I'm ___. |
| Soy de ___. / Vivo en ___. | I'm from ___. / I live in ___. |
| Por favor. / Gracias. / De nada. | Please. / Thank you. / You're welcome. |
| Perdón. / Lo siento. | Excuse me. / I'm sorry. |
| No entiendo. / ¿Puede repetir? | I don't understand. / Can you repeat? (formal) |
| Más despacio, por favor. | More slowly, please. |
| ¿Qué significa ___? / ¿Cómo se dice ___? | What does ___ mean? / How do you say ___? |
| ¿Dónde está el baño? | Where is the bathroom? |
| Quisiera ___, por favor. | I would like ___, please. |

### Question words

| Spanish | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ¿qué? | what? |
| ¿quién? / ¿quiénes? | who? / who (plural)? |
| ¿dónde? / ¿adónde? | where? / where to? |
| ¿de dónde? | from where? |
| ¿cuándo? | when? |
| ¿cómo? | how? |
| ¿cuánto/a/os/as? | how much? / how many? |
| ¿por qué? | why? |

Spanish questions use opening and closing marks: **¿ ... ?** Exclamations use **¡ ... !**

### Grammar at a glance

**Subject pronouns:** *yo* I, *tú* you (informal), *él/ella* he/she, *usted* you (formal), *nosotros/as* we, *ellos/ellas* they, *ustedes* you (plural). Subject pronouns are often omitted because the verb ending shows who is doing the action.

**Present tense patterns:**

| Infinitive | yo | tú | él/ella/usted | nosotros/as | ellos/ustedes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hablar (speak) | hablo | hablas | habla | hablamos | hablan |
| comer (eat) | como | comes | come | comemos | comen |
| vivir (live) | vivo | vives | vive | vivimos | viven |

**Core verbs:**

| Verb | Useful forms |
|---|---|
| ser (identity, origin, description) | soy, eres, es, somos, son |
| estar (state, location) | estoy, estás, está, estamos, están |
| tener (have; age) | tengo, tienes, tiene, tenemos, tienen |
| ir (go) | voy, vas, va, vamos, van |
| llamarse (be called) | me llamo, te llamas, se llama, nos llamamos, se llaman |

**Ser or estar?** *Soy estudiante* (identity); *Soy de Canadá* (origin); *Estoy cansado/a* (state); *Madrid está en España* (location). Learn common uses as phrases; the distinction has exceptions.

**Hay and estar:** *Hay un café aquí* = There's a café here (existence). *El café está aquí* = The café is here (location of a known thing).

**Articles and agreement:** *el libro / los libros*; *la casa / las casas*. Adjectives usually agree: *un chico alto*, *una chica alta*, *dos chicas altas*. Learn each noun with *el* or *la*; endings are clues, not guarantees.

**Negation:** Put *no* before the verb: *No hablo francés.*

**Likes:** *Me gusta el café* (I like coffee); *Me gustan los libros* (I like books). The verb agrees with the thing liked.

## The 30 days

### Week 1: Meet people and build the basics

**Day 1 | Sounds and first phrases**  
Learn the vowel sounds, silent *h*, *ñ*, and stress marks. Practice *hola, buenos días, gracias, por favor, sí, no, perdón*.  
**Say/write:** A 20-second greeting and introduction, even if you only know your name.  
**Exit check:** Say five greetings or polite phrases from memory.

**Day 2 | Names and *ser***  
Learn *ser* and *llamarse*: *Soy Alex. Me llamo Alex. ¿Cómo te llamas?* Practice *¿Quién eres?* and *¿Es usted ...?*  
**Say/write:** Introduce yourself and ask another person's name.  
**Exit check:** Give the *yo*, *tú*, and *él/ella* forms of *ser* and *llamarse*.

**Day 3 | Countries, languages, and *de***  
Learn countries, nationalities, and languages useful to you. Practice *Soy de ___. Hablo inglés y un poco de español. ¿De dónde eres?* Nationality words change for gender in many cases: *mexicano/mexicana*.  
**Say/write:** Introduce yourself, say where you're from, and name languages you speak.  
**Exit check:** Answer *¿De dónde eres?* and *¿Qué idiomas hablas?*

**Day 4 | Nouns, articles, and plurals**  
Learn *el/la/los/las* and plural forms: *el amigo / los amigos; la ciudad / las ciudades*. Add 10 everyday objects to your vocabulary, with articles.  
**Say/write:** Label objects around you and describe what you have using *Tengo ...* if you know it; otherwise use *Es ...*  
**Exit check:** Make five singular nouns plural and choose their articles.

**Day 5 | Numbers, age, and *tener***  
Learn numbers 0-20 and tens to 100. Practice *tener*: *tengo, tienes, tiene, tenemos, tienen*. Age uses *tener*: *Tengo 30 años.*  
**Say/write:** State your age (or invent one), ask someone's age, and say a phone number digit by digit.  
**Exit check:** Count 0-20 and make three sentences with *tener*.

**Day 6 | Questions and answers**  
Practice question words from the reference. Use rising intonation for yes/no questions: *¿Hablas español?* Common answers: *Sí, un poco. / No, todavía no.*  
**Say/write:** Write and answer six questions about name, origin, age, language, and location.  
**Exit check:** Ask three questions without looking at notes.

**Day 7 | Week 1 review**  
Review Days 1-6. Do the routine, then spend 20 extra minutes on anything you missed.  
**Checkpoint:** Give a 45-second introduction: name, origin, languages, age, and one thing you like. Ask and answer five basic questions. You should be able to do this using notes only for words you genuinely haven't learned yet.

### Week 2: Daily life and preferences

**Day 8 | Family and possessives**  
Learn family words: *madre, padre, hermana, hermano, hijo/a, pareja, familia*. Possessives: *mi/mis, tu/tus, su/sus*.  
**Say/write:** Describe your family or an imaginary one in 4-5 sentences: *Mi hermana se llama ...*  
**Exit check:** Use *mi, mis, tu,* and *su* in a sentence.

**Day 9 | Descriptions and *ser/estar***  
Learn common adjectives: *alto/a, bajo/a, joven, simpático/a, grande, pequeño/a, cansado/a, contento/a*. Review agreement.  
**Say/write:** Describe two people and how one of them feels today.  
**Exit check:** Make one identity/description sentence with *ser* and one feeling sentence with *estar*.

**Day 10 | Regular present-tense verbs**  
Practice *-ar, -er,* and *-ir* verbs. Useful verbs: *hablar, estudiar, trabajar, comer, beber, vivir, aprender*.  
**Say/write:** Describe your day using at least four verbs and two different subjects.  
**Exit check:** Conjugate *hablar, comer,* and *vivir* for *yo* and *nosotros/as*.

**Day 11 | Routine and time words**  
Learn *por la mañana, por la tarde, por la noche, todos los días, a veces, normalmente*. Practice clock time: *Es la una. Son las dos. A las ocho ...*  
**Say/write:** Describe a typical day in 5-6 simple sentences.  
**Exit check:** Say when you wake up, work/study, and go to bed.

**Day 12 | Days, dates, and schedules**  
Learn days of the week, months, and *hoy, mañana, ayer*. Practice *¿Qué día es hoy? ¿Qué fecha es? ¿A qué hora ...?*  
**Say/write:** Give today's date and describe two activities on different days.  
**Exit check:** Say the days of the week and make a simple appointment.

**Day 13 | Food and *gustar***  
Learn 10 foods/drinks you like. Practice *Me gusta ... / Me gustan ... / No me gusta ... / ¿Te gusta ...?*  
**Say/write:** Describe three likes, two dislikes, and ask someone about theirs.  
**Exit check:** Choose *gusta* or *gustan* for five foods/items.

**Day 14 | Week 2 review**  
Review Days 8-13; mix old and new vocabulary instead of rereading everything.  
**Checkpoint:** Speak for one minute about yourself, your family, your routine, and food you like. Write 6-8 sentences without translating word for word from English.

### Week 3: Getting around and practical needs

**Day 15 | Places, *hay,* and location**  
Learn places: *casa, escuela, trabajo, tienda, supermercado, restaurante, estación, banco*. Practice *hay* versus *estar*: *Hay un banco. El banco está cerca.*  
**Say/write:** Describe what is in your neighborhood and where two places are.  
**Exit check:** Make one sentence with *hay* and one with *está/están*.

**Day 16 | Location words and directions**  
Learn *a la derecha, a la izquierda, todo recto, cerca, lejos, al lado de, enfrente de, detrás de*.  
**Say/write:** Ask *¿Dónde está ...?* and give simple directions to a place.  
**Exit check:** Explain a route using three direction phrases.

**Day 17 | *Ir* and simple plans**  
Practice *ir*: *voy, vas, va, vamos, van*. Learn *ir a + place* and *ir a + infinitive*: *Voy al mercado. Vamos a estudiar.*  
**Say/write:** Say where you're going today and one thing you're going to do.  
**Exit check:** Make three *ir a* sentences with different subjects.

**Day 18 | Home and everyday objects**  
Learn rooms and objects: *cocina, baño, dormitorio, sala, mesa, silla, cama, puerta*. Use *mi casa tiene ...* and *hay ... en ...*  
**Say/write:** Describe your home or an imaginary room in 5 sentences.  
**Exit check:** Name five rooms/objects and place three in a room.

**Day 19 | Shopping and prices**  
Learn *¿Cuánto cuesta? ¿Cuánto cuestan? Quiero ... ¿Tiene ...? caro/a, barato/a, dinero, tarjeta, efectivo*. Review numbers to 100.  
**Say/write:** Role-play buying three items and asking their prices.  
**Exit check:** Ask the price of one item and understand a price you choose yourself.

**Day 20 | Ordering food politely**  
Practice *Quisiera ..., por favor. Para mí ... La cuenta, por favor. ¿Qué recomienda?* Learn a few menu words: *agua, café, té, pan, arroz, pollo, pescado, ensalada*.  
**Say/write:** Role-play ordering a drink and meal, asking for the bill, and saying thank you.  
**Exit check:** Complete a four-line customer/server exchange aloud.

**Day 21 | Week 3 review**  
Review Days 15-20. Use audio to practice catching familiar place, food, number, and direction words.  
**Checkpoint:** Role-play asking directions, buying an item, and ordering at a café. Then write a short message saying where you are going and when.

### Week 4: Put it together

**Day 22 | Weather and seasons**  
Learn *Hace calor/frío. Hace sol. Llueve. Nieva. Hace buen/mal tiempo.* Add seasons: *primavera, verano, otoño, invierno*.  
**Say/write:** Describe today's weather and what you wear or do in that weather.  
**Exit check:** Give three weather sentences and one question about the weather.

**Day 23 | Clothes and colors**  
Learn useful clothing and colors: *camisa, pantalones, zapatos, vestido, chaqueta; rojo/a, azul, verde, negro/a, blanco/a*. Practice *llevar* in common phrases: *Llevo una camisa azul.*  
**Say/write:** Describe what you're wearing and ask what another person is wearing.  
**Exit check:** Describe three clothing items with colors.

**Day 24 | Free time and abilities**  
Learn hobbies: *leer, escuchar música, ver películas, cocinar, caminar, bailar, jugar*. Practice *poder*: *puedo, puedes, puede, podemos, pueden* + infinitive.  
**Say/write:** Say what you like doing and what you can do. Ask someone about their hobbies.  
**Exit check:** Make two *puedo* sentences and ask one ability question.

**Day 25 | Needs and simple requests**  
Practice *necesitar* and *querer*: *Necesito ayuda. Quiero agua. ¿Puede ayudarme?* Review *por favor, gracias, no entiendo,* and *más despacio*.  
**Say/write:** Act out needing help in a shop or station; ask for clarification politely.  
**Exit check:** Make a request, say you don't understand, and ask for repetition.

**Day 26 | Connect sentences**  
Learn *y, pero, porque, también, muy, un poco*. Example: *Estudio español porque me gusta viajar.*  
**Say/write:** Turn short statements into a connected paragraph of 6-8 sentences about yourself.  
**Exit check:** Use *y, pero,* and *porque* correctly in original sentences.

**Day 27 | Listening and repair strategies**  
Use 2-3 minutes of beginner audio. Listen once without pausing, then again and note words you recognize. Practice asking for repetition, slower speech, or a meaning.  
**Say/write:** Summarize only what you understood using simple Spanish; partial understanding is the point.  
**Exit check:** Use three repair phrases without looking.

**Day 28 | Full A1 practice day**  
Review all four weeks. Do a 10-minute conversation practice with a tutor, language partner, or voice recording where you play both roles.  
**Checkpoint:** Introduce yourself, answer basic questions, describe routine and likes, ask for food or directions, and say what you plan to do. Keep the conversation simple and ask for help when needed.

**Day 29 | Find and repair gaps**  
Do the final self-check below. Mark every missed item. Spend the session reviewing only those items, then retry without notes.  
**Say/write:** Record a 90-second introduction and everyday-life description. Write a matching paragraph of 8-10 sentences.

**Day 30 | A1 simulation and next steps**  
Repeat the Day 28 role-play without notes. Read a short beginner passage and listen to a short beginner clip; write down the main idea and details you catch.  
**Finish line:** You can introduce yourself, exchange basic personal information, understand familiar words in clear speech, and handle a short rehearsed everyday interaction. Note what still feels hard and choose it as next month's focus.

## Final self-check

Try these without notes. Mark **yes**, **almost**, or **not yet**. “Not yet” is a study target, not a failure.

- [ ] I can greet someone, introduce myself, and ask their name.
- [ ] I can say where I am from, where I live, what languages I speak, and basic personal details.
- [ ] I can understand and use numbers, days, dates, and simple times.
- [ ] I can describe my family, a typical day, a few preferences, and familiar places.
- [ ] I can ask and answer simple questions with *qué, quién, dónde, cuándo,* and *cuánto*.
- [ ] I can use common present-tense forms of *ser, estar, tener, ir,* and regular verbs.
- [ ] I can order something, ask a price, ask for directions, and request repetition.
- [ ] I can understand the main point of a short, clear beginner audio or conversation on a familiar topic.
- [ ] I can write 5-8 simple connected sentences about myself.

**Keep going:** Repeat the days where you marked “not yet,” add 10 minutes of conversation practice daily, and keep listening to comprehensible beginner Spanish. Consistency and understanding matter more than racing through grammar.
