# Relief
## Coast Ranges
Part of Pacific mountain system. Elevations in Oregon are generally below 2,000 feet but Mount Bolivar, east of Port Orford, reaches 4,319 feet. The Klamath Mountains extends southward for about 250 miles from the foothills south of the Oregon Willamette Valley in to the northwestern side of the Central Valley. Mount Ashland is the tallest of the Oregon Klamath Mountains at 7,532 feet. The Siskiyou Mountains (part of Klamath Mountains) include the Oregon Caves National Monument (3.5 miles of caves system).
![[oregon_costal_ranges.png]]
## Cascade Range
A broad lava plateau that is part of the Pacific mountain system. Extends northward for more than 700 miles and lie 100 to 150 miles inland from the Pacific Ocean and east of the Willamette Valley. Includes Mount Hood (11,235 feet, highest point in Oregon) and Mount Jefferson (10,497 feet, second highest in Oregon). Most of the summits are extinct volcanoes and are marked by glaciation, stream dissection, and are heavily wooded.

## Blue-Wallowa Mountains
Blue Mountains have an average elevation of about 6,500 ft. Includes Aldrich, Strawberry, and Elkhorn ranges. Highest peak is Rock Creek Butte (9,105 ft), on the Elkhorn Ridge. Wallowa Mountains are east of the La Grande and Baker valleys.

# Drainage
- **Columbia River**: largest river flowing into the Pacific Ocean from North America. One of the world’s greatest sources of hydroelectric power (a third of the potential hydropower of the US).
- **Rogue River**: cuts through and provides the major drainage for the Klamath Mountains
- **Willamette Valley**: an alluvial plain that contains the prime agriculture land of the state and separates the moist coastal region from the arid interior. Includes the Willamette River.
- **Owyhee River**: Owyhee Dam (1932) impounds Lake Owyhee
- **Snake River**: forms the boundary between Idaho and Oregon for 216 miles. Lower Snake River, from Weiser to the mouth, flows through a gorge 1 mile known as Hells Canyon, the deepest river gorge in North America.
- **Malheur River**: rising in the Strawberry Mountain Wilderness on the southern slopes of the Blue Mountains in the Malheur National Forest. Warm Springs Reservoir, impounded by Warm Springs Dam (1919), is on the Middle Fork of the Malheur, north of Riverside.
- **Lakes**: Upper Klamath Lake, Crater Lake
