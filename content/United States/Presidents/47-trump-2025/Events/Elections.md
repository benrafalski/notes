
# Partisan Redistricting
## Missouri
- In September 2026, The [Missouri Supreme Court blocked](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/blow-trump-missouri-top-court-blocks-republican-drawn-congressional-map-2026-09-03/) a new Republican congressional map from being used. The court ruled 7-0 that the map must be approved by voters ​in a November referendum before it can take effect. The state held primary elections under the new map in August, and the court did not specify how it expected nominees to be selected now that the old map is back in place. 
- A few days later, U.S. Supreme Court justice [Kavanaugh declined](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-supreme-court-wont-reinstate-republican-drawn-congressional-map-missouri-2026-09-08/) to reinstate the Republican-drawn congressional map in an emergency ruling, even despite Chief U.S. District Judge Stephen Clark acting in a separate case ruling in favor of a TRO that the reconfigured boundaries must be used. However, the Supreme Court also later [blocked Judge Clark's ruling](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-supreme-court-blocks-republican-drawn-congressional-map-missouri-2026-09-10/) as well.
- On September 21, the St. Louis, Missouri-based 8th US Circuit [Court of Appeals ruled in favor](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/us-appeals-court-backs-missouris-republican-drawn-congressional-map-sets-up-2026-09-21/) of the Republican drawn map (upholding Clark's ruling). However, on September 25, the Supreme Court for a [third time](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-supreme-court-bars-republican-drawn-map-missouri-third-time-2026-09-25/) ruled unanimously to strike down the map

# Illegal Voting
- **Claim**: non-citizen voting is widespread and part of an organized conspiracy led by Democrats
- **Facts**:
	- Since 1996,  just [129 people have been prosecuted](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/trump-loses-21-straight-court-battles-state-voter-rolls-midterms-near-2026-08-07/#:~:text=A%20Reuters%20investigation%20found%20that%20just%20129%20people%20have%20been%20prosecuted%20for%20noncitizen%20voting%20since%20a%20law%20criminalizing%20it%20was%20passed%20in%201996%2C%20and%20that%20most%20of%20those%20cases%20stemmed%20from%20voter%20confusion%20or%20miscommunication%20with%20election%20officials%20rather%20than%20any%20conspiracy.) for noncitizen voting, with most of those cases stemming from voter confusion or miscommunication with election officials rather than any conspiracy.
	- US states may have added more than [30,000 self-declared non-citizens to the nation’s voter rolls](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/thirty-thousand-mistakes-how-red-blue-states-added-non-citizens-us-voter-rolls-2026-09-23/) since 2000 because of software glitches and clerical errors made while processing their driver's license and state ID applications. The data showed errors occurred in California, Illinois, Iowa, Nevada, Ohio, Oregon, Pennsylvania, South ⁠Dakota, Texas, Utah and Virginia. These numbers suggest inadvertent bureaucratic failures, ‌rather than organized efforts to register immigrants illegally, are a major source of non-citizen registrations, and cause doubt about whether requiring proof of citizenship for voter registration would eliminate the issue.
- **Midterm Prosecutions**: In September 2026 ahead of the midterms, the DOJ brought 'voting by aliens' cases at an unprecedented rate with the White House calling it [a 'tier one' priority](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/trump-ramps-up-noncitizen-voting-prosecutions-ahead-midterms-2026-09-23/). In September, the DOJ charged 20 people for "voting by aliens" and mobilized hundreds of DHS agents in all 50 states to aid in the DOJ effort. DOJ official also told senior prosecutors at US attorney’s offices across the country that prosecutors could no longer decide on their own to refuse election fraud cases (it has traditionally been career prosecutors who decide whether charges ​are justified in such cases). Since Trump returned to office, prosecutors have accused [at least 147 people](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/trump-expands-hunt-noncitizen-voters-with-broad-subpoena-campaign-before-2026-10-28/) of illegally voting under a federal “voting by aliens” statute, including 102 since the start of September
- **Pressuring States**: On November 2nd, the day before the election, US District Judge Tanya Chutkan (DC-based appointee of former Obama) [temporarily barred](https://www.reuters.com/world/judge-blocks-trump-administration-pressuring-states-over-noncitizen-voting-2026-11-03/) the DOJ and the DHS from sending states lists of registered voters the administration says may not be citizens, and from asking state election officials to separate out those voters' ballots or not count them until citizenship is proven. Chutkan said the administration's data may not be reliable, meaning there was a risk that lawfully registered voters would be disenfranchised.

On October 28, US District Judge David Carter [rejected the DOJ request](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-justice-dept-loses-initial-bid-block-votes-alleged-noncitizens-california-2026-10-29/) to temporarily block California from counting their mail in ballots of the 20,000 voters and told agency lawyers the "court believes that it would be wise that ​any threats to state and county election employees cease immediately."

## Medicaid cuts
 In October 2026 US District Judge Richard Stearns in Boston (Clinton appointee) rules in [preliminary injunction](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/us-judge-blocks-trump-limits-medicaid-work-requirement-exceptions-2026-10-29/) blocking Trump from enforcing new work requirements for Medicaid. The work requirements had been enacted under Trump's signature spending bill, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. CMS's rule provided guidance to states on how to implement the work requirements including an exemption for individuals who are "medically frail or otherwise have special medical needs." Stearns said CMS's rule unlawfully limited who qualifies as "medically ‌frail" ⁠by dictating that the exemption only applies if someone's "physical, mental, or other behavioral health condition significantly impairs the individual’s ability to comply with the community engagement requirement.” Stearns struck down that portion ​of the rule but upheld two others, including one barring states from ​relying on proof ⁠of patients' medical frailty that is more than 12 months old.
