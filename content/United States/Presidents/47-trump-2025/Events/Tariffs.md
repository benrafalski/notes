# "Liberation Day" (IEEPA Tariffs)
## De Minimis  
- August 13: U.S. Court of International Trade rules [in ‌favor](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/us-court-backs-trumps-power-close-de-minimis-tariff-exemption-2026-08-13/) of Trump's 2025 decision to rescind a "de minimis" exemption for tariffs on some imports of less than $800. ​Trump used IEEPA to close the exemption for ⁠low-value imports from China, Mexico and Canada but the Supreme Court decision didn't strike them down. However, Congress closed the “de minimis" exemption through its passage of the omnibus tax-and-spending bill in 2025, but the legislative action ​kept the exemption in place until July 2027.

# Canada Tariffs
## Trade Talks
In June 2025, Trump abruptly [cut off trade talks](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/tariff-deals-could-be-wrapped-up-by-labor-day-bessent-says-talks-pick-up-2025-06-27/) with Canada, saying that Canada's newly enacted digital services tax on U.S. technology firms was "a direct and blatant attack on our country." Two days later, Canada [scrapped the new digital tax](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/us-restart-trade-negotiations-with-canada-immediately-white-house-says-2025-06-30/) and the US restarted trade talks right after.
### Reagan Anti-Tariff Ad
- In October 2025, Trump [again cuts off trade talks](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/trump-says-all-trade-talks-with-canada-are-terminated-2025-10-24/) with Canada over an anti-tariff advertisement issued by Ontario's provincial government, that featured Ronald Reagan out of context saying tariffs cause trade wars and economic disaster. 
- Ontario Premier Doug Ford said he ordered the ads to keep running during the weekend's baseball World Series games between the Toronto Blue Jays and Los Angeles Dodgers to reach a bigger U.S. audience but would halt the ads after speaking with PM Carney.
- White House adviser Kevin Hassett said Trump was frustrated with Canada and trade talks have not been going well, with the ad likely being a pre-text to cut off talks again. 
- The Ronald Reagan Presidential Foundation said the ad used "selective audio and video."
- The ad's voiceover consists of Reagan criticizing tariffs saying they cause job losses and trade wars, by using five complete sentences from a 1987 five-minute weekly address, spliced together out of sequence. 
- The ad didn't mention that Reagan was using the address to explain that tariffs imposed on Japan by his administration should be seen as a sadly unavoidable exception to his basic belief in free trade as the key to prosperity, due to Japan's unfair trade practices at the time. 
- However, Reagan today would likely be against Trump's broad tariff regime to bring US manufacturing back.
## Fentanyl Tariffs
In August 2025 Trump [raised the rate](https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/trump-hits-dozens-more-countries-with-steep-tariffs-2025-07-31/) on Canadian goods subject to fentanyl-related tariffs to 35%, from 25% previously, saying Canada had "failed to cooperate" in curbing illicit narcotics flows into the U.S.
### Congressional Pushback
In February 2026, the House voted 219-211, passing a resolution to [terminate](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/us-house-backs-bid-end-canada-tariffs-rare-rebuke-trump-2026-02-11/) Trump's use of a national emergency to impose tariffs on Canada. Representative Gregory Meeks of New York, who introduced the resolution, said it was about lowering the cost of living for American families, disputing claims that there was a drug-related national emergency as Trump had claimed to justify tariffs against Canada.
## Tariff Threats
### Canada-China Ties
In January 2026: Trump [threatens 100% tariffs](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/trump-threatens-canada-with-100-tariff-over-possible-deal-with-china-2026-01-24/) on Canada if it follows through on a trade deal with China. Earlier in the month, Carney traveled to Canada to reset the countries' strained relationship and reached a trade deal with Canada's second-biggest trading partner after the U.S. Immediately after Carney's China trip, Trump sounded supportive, saying "It's a good thing for him to sign a trade deal ... If you can get a deal with China, you should do that." However, Trump changed his mind after Carney spoke out against Trump's pursuit of Greenland at the WEF in Davos, calling on nations to accept that a rules-based global order was over and pointed to Canada as an example of how "middle powers" might act together to avoid being victimized by American hegemony.
### Aircraft Tariffs
In January 2026 Trump said U.S. will decertify Bombardier Global Express business jets and [threatened 50% import tariffs](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/trump-says-us-decertifying-bombardier-global-express-until-canada-certifies-2026-01-30/) on all aircraft made in Canada until they certified a number of planes produced by U.S. rival Gulfstream. The White House said Trump was not decertifying Canadian-built planes currently in operation. There are 150 Global Express aircraft in service registered in the U.S., operated by 115 operators and 5,425 total aircraft of various types made in Canada in service registered in the U.S. It does not appear the FAA has the legal authority to revoke certifications for planes based on economic reasons, as it can only do so for safety reasons under existing regulations.
## Section 338 Tariffs
In July 2026 Trump [imposes new 50% tariffs](https://www.reuters.com/business/us-imposes-new-50-tariffs-canadian-products-2026-07-20/) on $20 billion worth of Canadian products using Section 338 of the 1930 Tariff Act in response to what the his admin called its discriminatory treatment of American-made cars, alcohol and dairy goods. In August, Trump announces [three-day pause](https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/carney-trump-speak-ahead-50-us-tariff-deadline-2026-08-18/) on the Canada 50% tariffs set to go into effect at midnight, saying the two countries had reached a deal.
