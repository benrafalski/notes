# Remaking Washington
## Kennedy Center
### Kennedy Center Overhaul
- In December 2025, the Trump [installed](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/kennedy-center-new-years-eve-concerts-canceled-after-addition-trumps-name-2025-12-30/) board renamed the Kennedy Center to the The Donald J. Trump and The John F. Kennedy Memorial Center for the Performing Arts, or Trump Kennedy Center for short.
- February 1, 2026: Trump announces that the Kennedy Center with be [closed for two years](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/kennedy-center-cease-entertainment-operations-two-years-trump-says-2026-02-01/) starting on July 4 so he can renovate it to his liking.
- August 13: Trump installed Kennedy Center board [votes again to add Trump’s name to building](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/kennedy-center-board-votes-inscribe-trumps-name-building-2026-08-13/), rename physical site for Trump and votes to close center for two years for renovations despite Judge Cooper's court order saying, "The Kennedy Center’s organic statute makes crystal clear that the Center is to be named for President Kennedy, and it cannot bear any other formal name or public memorial based ​on the Board’s unilateral say-so." Cooper also said, however, that the board has "wide discretion" to make ​operational decisions ⁠including potentially closing it for renovations.
#### Court Battle Timeline
- May 29: Trump to transfer control of the Kennedy Center to Congress, after U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper in Washington ordered the [removal of Trump's name](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-judge-orders-removal-trumps-name-kennedy-center-2026-05-29/) from the iconic Washington venue and blocked his plans to close it for renovations.
- June 4: Kennedy Center [orders staff](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/kennedy-center-remove-trump-name-after-court-decision-2026-06-04/) to remove Trump's name from the institution to comply with a Judge Cooper's order.
- June 13: Trump's name [removed](https://www.reuters.com/world/judge-keeps-order-place-remove-trumps-name-kennedy-center-2026-06-12/) from Kennedy Center after removal work began around 1:20 a.m. after a missed two-week-old deadline from the court order due to "bad weather". The removal was forced after both Judge Cooper and a federal appeals court both rejected Trump's bid to pause the removal order.
### Cancelled Performances
   - Following the move, both the Christmas and New Year's Eve concerts were cancelled after several performers withdrew in protest. 
   - In January 2026, the [Washington National Opera](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/washington-national-opera-latest-leave-kennedy-center-after-trump-takeover-2026-01-10/) left the Kennedy Center after a collapse in box office revenue and "shattered" donor confidence in the wake of Trump's takeover.
   - Also in late January, Grammy Award-winning opera star [Renee Fleming](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/opera-star-renee-fleming-withdraws-kennedy-center-may-performances-2026-01-24/) withdrew from performances in May at the Kennedy Center. In 2025, Fleming left her role as artistic adviser-at-large at the center, citing the departures of the institution's previous leadership. 
   - The following week American composer [Philip Glass](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/composer-philip-glass-drops-kennedy-center-world-premiere-over-trump-takeover-2026-01-27/) withdrew the world premiere of his symphony "Lincoln" from the Kennedy Center, saying the work's values conflict with those of the performing arts venue.

## Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool
- **Renovation Failure**: In late June 2026, Trump said much of the water at the newly renovated Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool in Washington "probably" has to be drained for repairs following what [he called "vandalism"](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/trump-blaming-alleged-vandals-says-washington-reflecting-pool-needs-repairs-2026-06-21/) of the site. Trump did not provide evidence to support his allegation of vandalism, which he said included someone pouring corrosive chemicals into the pool. Trump announced on June 6 that work was completed on a $14.7 million renovation project for the pool but soon after, workers started pouring hydrogen peroxide into the pool to combat an algae bloom that had turned it green. Following that, the paint on the reflecting pool was peeling away from the bottom and into the algae-tinted water. The Trump admin later placed Reflecting Pool [under surveillance](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/reflecting-pool-now-under-surveillance-trump-blames-vandals-green-algae-2026-06-25/) to back Trump's "vandals" claims.
- **Ex-US Olympian Arrested**: In early July 2026, Ex-US Olympian canoeist David Hearn was [indicted](https://www.reuters.com/world/ex-us-olympian-indicted-over-alleged-reflecting-pool-vandalism-2026-07-02/) over alleged Reflecting Pool vandalism the DOJ said caused more than $1,000 in damage to pool. Hearn previously acknowledged reaching into the pool while cycling in the area and told U.S. media outlets that he tugged on a partially detached piece of liner, but did not remove anything. However, by the end of July, the DOJ abruptly [dropped their case](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-justice-department-drops-case-against-former-us-olympian-over-lincoln-2026-07-31/) against Hearn. In the filing, the DOJ admitted that a 'botched installation' caused the Reflecting Pool's new liner to peel, not vandals as they originally claimed and also blamed the Interior Department for "slowly ⁠trickling information" to prosecutors about the renovation. Trump said "[I disagree 100%](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/trump-disputes-his-own-justice-departments-findings-reflecting-pool-2026-08-01/)" on the DOJ decision and continued his unsubstantiated vandalism claims. The White House later [asked the DOJ](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/trump-white-house-asked-doj-explore-new-reflecting-pool-charges-sources-say-2026-08-11/) to reopen dropped Reflecting Pool charges, in the latest example of the ​White House intervening in criminal investigations, breaking from decades-long DOJ practice.

### Indirect Research
- **DOD Funds**: Twelve universities and three academic trade groups, including ​the Association of American Universities , sued the DOD in May 2025 after the department said ​it would cap reimbursed for "indirect ⁠costs" of funded research at 15%. U.S. District Judge Brian Murphy in ‌Boston, declared the reimbursement cap illegal in December and further awarded [$450,000 in legal fees](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/us-must-pay-university-groups-legal-fees-case-over-pentagon-research-funds-2026-08-05/) to the AAU since the DOD implemented the policy after judges already ruled against similar policies in the NIH and DOE.

##### Court Case
Case: [President and Fellows of Harvard College v. US Department of Health and Human Services (1:25-cv-11048)](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69921962/president-and-fellows-of-harvard-college-v-us-department-of-health-and/?page=2)
- Timeline
	- April 21, 2025: [Harvard sued Trump](https://www.reuters.com/legal/harvard-university-sues-trump-administration-media-reports-say-2025-04-21/), saying his funding freeze to them was illegal and violated their First Amendment right to speech.
	- September 3: U.S. District Judge Allison Burroughs in Boston ruled that Trump [unlawfully terminated](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/trump-administration-unlawfully-cut-harvards-funding-us-judge-rules-2025-09-03/) $2.2 billion in grants to Harvard. Burroughs said that while Harvard had tolerated hateful behavior for too long, the Trump admin "used antisemitism as a smokescreen for a targeted, ideologically-motivated assault on this country's premier universities." She said the grant terminations were in retaliation against the school in violation of its First Amendment free speech rights. Burroughs said it was the job of courts to safeguard academic freedom and "ensure that important research is not improperly subjected to arbitrary and procedurally infirm grant terminations, even if doing so risks the wrath of a government committed to its agenda no matter the cost." She barred the administration from terminating or freezing any additional federal funding to Harvard and blocked it from continuing to withhold payment on existing grants or refusing to award new funding to the school in the future.
