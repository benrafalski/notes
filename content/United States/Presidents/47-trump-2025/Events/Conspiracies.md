# Epstein
## DOJ Client List Memo
- In February 2025, far-right twitter influencers were invited to the White House and provided with binders marked "[The Epstein Files: Phase 1](https://apnews.com/article/jeffrey-epstein-justice-department-pam-bondi-03fbcd024f631440f7ed62b3c6927db3)" and "Declassified" that contained documents that had largely already been in the public domain, leading to internet backlash. After the release flopped, in a March TV interview, Bondi claimed the Biden administration "sat on these documents, no one did anything with them," adding: "Sadly these people don't believe in transparency, but I think more unfortunately, I think a lot of them don't believe in honesty." On July 7, the DOJ said Epstein didn't have a client list and that no more files related to the wealthy financier's sex trafficking investigation would be made public despite promises from Attorney General Pam Bondi that had raised the expectations of conservative influencers and conspiracy theorists. Bondi suggesting in a Fox News interview earlier that year that such a document was "sitting on my desk" for review. FBI Director Kash Patel and Deputy Director Dan Bongino have also insisted in television and podcast interviews that the evidence was clear that Epstein had killed himself. They also released a video from outside Epstein's jail cell as proof he actually killed himself. Bondi cited the existence of "tens of thousands" of videos that she said showed Epstein "with children or child porn" as the reason for no more public releases, despite multiple people who participated in the criminal cases of Epstein and socialite former girlfriend Ghislaine Maxwell saying they had not seen and did not know of a trove of recordings along the lines of what Bondi had referenced. Indictments and detention memos also don't allege the existence of video recordings and neither Epstein nor Maxwell were charged with possession of child sex abuse material even though that would have been easier for prosecutors to prove than the sex trafficking counts they faced.
- After the July 7 memo, Trump had to deal with the [fallout on Twitter](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/trump-white-house-race-stem-epstein-conspiracy-fallout-2025-07-16/) from his conspiracy brained followers, who were calling for Bondi to be fired. Trump chose to stand with Bondi, saying "I don't understand why the Jeffrey Epstein case is of interest to anybody," and urging his followers to just move on.
- On July 16, Trump said his supporters who were mad about Epstein were "weaklings" that were falling for a "[Democrat hoax](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/trump-says-supporters-questioning-jeffrey-epstein-case-are-weaklings-2025-07-16/)."
## Grand Jury Testimony
- On July 17, Trump asked for a [release of grand jury testimony](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/trump-asks-release-grand-jury-documents-epstein-case-2025-07-18/) from the Epstein case but also said he would [not recommend appointing a special prosecutor](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/trump-will-not-recommend-special-prosecutor-epstein-case-2025-07-17/) on the case. U.S. District Judge Robin Rosenberg in Florida on July 23 denied the administration's request to unseal records from the grand jury investigations in 2005 and 2007 finding that the request did not fall into any of the limited exceptions that may allow for the release of such material. 
- Manhattan-based U.S. District Judges Richard Berman and Paul Engelmayer responded to the DOJ and asked them to flesh out the [legal bases for the requests](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/trump-administration-asks-judges-release-epstein-maxwell-grand-jury-transcripts-2025-07-30/). On July 30, the DOJ responded saying unsealing the materials would be appropriate given the "abundant public interest" in the Epstein case and persistent scrutiny of how it was handled by federal law enforcement.
