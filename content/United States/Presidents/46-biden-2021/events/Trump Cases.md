- Case:

[[1](https://www.politifact.com/article/2023/apr/05/trumps-indictment-explained-falsifying-business-re/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)]

		- made the payment through a shell corporation he set up and funded at a bank in Manhattan
	- After the election, Trump reimbursed Cohen through a series of monthly checks, first from a Trump trust connected to his business and then from Trump’s bank account.
	- Each check was processed by the Trump Organization and the records produced were false.
	
- Catch and kill scheme: [[1](https://www.politifact.com/article/2023/apr/05/trumps-indictment-explained-falsifying-business-re/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)]
	- The National Enquirer tabloid worked with Trump to keep "damaging" stories about Trump from being published before the 2016 presidential election
	- American Media, the company that owned the National Enquirer, said in a non-prosecution agreement that it paid a source of a story to ensure that the source "did not publicize damaging allegations" about Trump before the election that might swing the outcome.
	- In August 2015, two months after Trump announced his candidacy, Trump met with Cohen and the American Media CEO (David Pecker) at Trump Tower.
		- Pecker agreed to watch for negative stories about Trump and alert Cohen before publishing.
	- In summer of 2017, Trump "invited the (American Media) CEO to the White House for a dinner to thank him for his help during the campaign,"
	- Stories:
		- In fall 2015, American Media agreed to pay $30,000 for a story, peddled by a former Trump Tower doorman, that alleged Trump had fathered a child out of wedlock.
			- The company later learned the story was false.
	- In June 2016, the editor-in-chief of the National Enquirer and American Media’s chief content officer contacted Cohen about an unnamed woman, believed to be McDougal, who said she had a sexual relationship with Trump. American Media paid the woman $150,000 for her silence and for two cover features about her.
		- In a September 2016 conversation captured on audio, Trump and Cohen discussed how to obtain the rights to the unnamed woman’s account from American Media and reimburse American Media for its payment.
		- On the recording, Trump asked, "So what do we got to pay for this? One fifty?" and suggested paying by cash. Trump mentioned payment by check and Cohen created a shell company and later paid the woman $125,000.
- Each of the 34 counts represents a check, invoice, and ledger entry that was falsified and that the grand jury found to have contained a "critical false statement" related to the payments.
- Prosecutors alleged Trump and others made the false business entries with the intent of committing, aiding, or concealing election law crimes.
	- New York law makes it a crime to influence an election to public offense by unlawful means.
	- Trump's lawyer made the hush-money payments to keep Stormy Daniels quiet and increase Trump's chances of winning the 2016 presidential election.
- Penalties:
	- Falsifying business records is a Class A misdemeanor in New York
	- The crime becomes a felony if the defendant made the false statements (or omissions) with the intent to commit, conceal, or aid another crime.
	- In that situation, the crime becomes a Class E felony with a possible penalty of one to four years in prison.

		- Falsifying business records in the second degree (§175.05) is a misdemeanor
		- Falsifying business records in the first degree (§175.10) is a felony if the defendant falsified records with intent to commit another crime, or aid or conceal another crime.
