# Overview
- Signed by US, USSR, and UK
- Signed in Moscow on August 5, 1963
- Banned all tests of nuclear weapons except those conducted underground.

# Background
- In 1955, there was worldwide public concern over the danger posed by atmospheric radioactive fallout produced by the aboveground testing of nuclear weapons. 
	- There were multiple proposals to ban testing but each one failed
- During 1959, both the US and USSR temporarily suspended their testing
- Between 1959-1961, US-Soviet negotiations slowed due to renewed Cold War tensions 
- Cuban missile crisis in 1962, the two sides became more motivated to real a deal again due to nuclear war fears
- By late 1962 Anglo-American and Soviet proposals for a draft treaty were close to one another

# Agreement
- After 10 days of discussion in Moscow in July–August 1963, representatives of the three nuclear powers pledged themselves for an “unlimited duration” to conduct no more tests in the atmosphere, underwater, or in space.
- The treaty 
	- **What it did do**: banned tests in the atmosphere, in outer space, and underwater but permitted underground testing and required no control posts, no on-site inspection, and no international supervisory body
	- **What it didn't do**: did not reduce nuclear stockpiles, halt the production of nuclear weapons, or restrict their use in time of war
- Amendments
	- United States, the United Kingdom, and the Soviet Union all have power to veto new amendments 
	- Any amendment must be approved by a majority of all the signatory states, including all three of the original parties.


# Aftermath
- Continued support
	- Soon after original treaty, it was signed by more than 100 other governments
	- Exceptions: France and China
- Expansion
	- Peaceful Nuclear Explosions Treaty (1976): US, UK, USSR agreed to banning "peaceful nuclear explosions" (those conducted for purposes of civil engineering projects)
	- Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty Negotiations (1977-1982): would extend the ban to underground tests but negotiations eventually abandoned by Reagan
	- 1991: USSR announces moratorium on future nuclear tests, and the U.S. Congress required that this be reciprocated and that talks on a treaty resume
	- Ad Hoc Committee on a Nuclear Test Ban (1994): 
		- UN General Assembly Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty draft (1996): to enter into force the treaty had to be ratified by all the nuclear powers and by 44 members of the Conference on Disarmament that possess nuclear reactors
		- By 2007 all of these countries but three (India, Pakistan, and North Korea) had signed, though 10 of those that had signed had not ratified, including the United States and China.
- Future Testing
	- After moratoriums of the early 1990s
		- Russia, the United States, the United Kingdom, and France conducted no further tests. 
		- France resumed testing briefly in 1995 but permanently ended testing the next year 
		- China conducted its last test on July 29, 1996
		- In 1998 both India and Pakistan tested nuclear weapons for the first time, although they also followed their tests with an informal moratorium
		- In October 2006 North Korea conducted a test of a nuclear device (had very small yield)
