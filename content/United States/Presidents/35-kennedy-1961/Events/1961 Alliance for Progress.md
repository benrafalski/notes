# Overview
- International economic development program created by the US for 22 Latin American countries
- Established in August 1961 under the Charter of Punta del Este (Uruguay)
- Also called Alianza

# Objectives
- Democratic government 
- Economic and social development
- Sustained growth in per capita income
- More equitable distribution of income
- Accelerated development in industry/agriculture
- Agrarian reform
- Improvement of health and welfare
- Stabilization of export prices 
- Domestic price stability

# Funding
- Seed funding
	- Estimated $20 billion needed in first 10 years
	- Half from the United States and the rest from international lending agencies and private sources.
- Inter-American Committee on the Alliance for Progress (CIAP): created in 1963 to serve as the coordinating agent between the international community and the Latin American countries involved

# Legacy
- Failure: some new schools, hospitals, and other physical plants but massive land reform was not achieved and population more than kept pace with gains in health and welfare. 
- Reason: U.S. aid decreased over the years, and political tensions between the United States and Latin America increased.
- Dissolution: dissolved in 1973 by the Organization of American States
