# Overview
- In last year of Eisenhower admin, CIA trained anticommunist Cuban exiles for a Cuban invasion
- Kennedy's Joint Chiefs of Staff unanimously advised him that this force, once ashore, would spark a general uprising against the Fidel Castro.
- Ended up being a fiasco with every man on the beachhead either killed or captured
- Kennedy assumed “sole responsibility” for the setback and privately told his father he would never accept a Joint Chiefs recommendation without first challenging it.
# Background
- Following Castro’s overthrow of Fulgencio Batista’s dictatorship in Cuba (January 1959), Cuba-US relations began to deteriorate.
- Castro's government confiscated private property (much of it owned by North American interests), sent agents to initiate revolutions in several Latin American countries, and established diplomatic and economic ties with leading socialist powers.
- By June 1959 Congress passed legislation enabling Eisenhower to take retaliatory steps
	- US cut off sugar purchases from Cuba and soon thereafter placed an embargo on all exports to Cuba except food and medicine.
- In May 1960, the CIA began planning an invasion of Cuba
- Right before Kennedy took over, Eisenhower in January 1961 broke diplomatic ties with Cuba
# Invasion
- When: April 17, 1961
- Where: Bahía de Cochinos (Bay of Pigs), or Playa Girón (Girón Beach) to Cubans
![[bay_of_pigs.png]]
- Who: some 1,500 Cuban exiles opposed to Fidel Castro
- How: invasion was financed and directed by the U.S. government
### Details
- April 15: three U.S.-made airplanes piloted by Cubans bombed Cuban air bases
- April 17: US trained Cubans and using U.S. equipment landed at several sites (principal landing at the Bay of Pigs)
- April 19: invasion force loses final stronghold and more than 1,100 total men captured
### Aftermath
- Critics aid the CIA with supplying faulty information to Kennedy
- In spite of Kennedy’s orders, supporters of Batista were included in the invasion force, whereas members of the noncommunist People’s Revolutionary Movement (most capable anti-Castro group) were excluded.
- From May 1961, Kennedy unofficially backed attempts to ransom the prisoners, but the efforts of the Tractors for Freedom Committee, headed by Eleanor Roosevelt, failed to raise the $28,000,000 needed for heavy-construction equipment demanded by Castro as reparations.
- Castro eventually released the prisoners  in exchange for $53,000,000 worth of food and medicine following negotiations with James B. Donovan.
- Between December 1962 - July 1965 the survivors were returned.
- Incident was crucial to the development of the Cuban missile crisis (October 1962)
