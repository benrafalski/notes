# Overview
- In last year of Eisenhower admin, CIA trained anticommunist Cuban exiles for a Cuban invasion
- Kennedy's Joint Chiefs of Staff unanimously advised him that this force, once ashore, would spark a general uprising against the Fidel Castro.
- Ended up being a fiasco with every man on the beachhead either killed or captured
- Kennedy assumed “sole responsibility” for the setback and privately told his father he would never accept a Joint Chiefs recommendation without first challenging it.

# People and Groups
## CIA 
### Frank Bender (Mr. B)
- The CIA Handler
- Pseudonym of Gerry Droller, a German-born CIA officer tasked with managing the political landscape of the Cuban exiles in Miami.
- Was a OSS veteran of WWII espionage who had worked with French resistance
- CIA sent him to Miami in June 1960 to unite the anti-Castro factions into the Frente despite him not speaking a word of Spanish
- Notorious for being abrasive, deeply manipulative, and playing different Cuban exile factions against one another
## Brigade 2506
1,400-man amphibious assault force that landed at the Bay of Pigs.
### Manuel Artime
- Political and civilian chief of Brigade 2506
- Devout Catholic and a doctor who originally fought with Castro and was given an agricultural post in the new government after the revolution. However, he quickly resigned because of Castro's communist trajectory. 
- He went underground, co-founded the MRP with Ray, and eventually fled to the United States.
- Unlike Manuel Ray, Artime was a right-of-center conservative backed by Catholic student groups, making him immensely popular with Bender, who sidelined Ray and elevated Artime to the leadership of the entire invasion
## Cuban Revolutionary Council (CRC)
Corps members included Tony Verona, Manuel Ray, Cardona, Carrillo.
### José Miró Cardona
### Justo Carrillo

## El Frente (FRD)
Formed in May 1960 in Mexico, served as an umbrella coalition of five major Cuban anti-communist, pro-democracy exile groups (MRP, ...). Its military wing became Brigade 2506. While the CIA used the Frente to legitimize the operation and recruit fighters, American handlers kept the Frente's political leadership almost entirely in the dark about the exact date, logistics, and planning of the invasion. This led to heavy resentment from Frente leaders, who felt that a Cuban liberation force should not be entirely controlled by foreign intelligence.
### Manuel Antonio "Tony" de Varona
- Before the revolution, Varona was a highly influential, traditional Cuban statesman (Prime Minister of Cuba from 1948 to 1950 and President of the Cuban Senate from 1950 to 1952).
- When Batista seized power in a 1952 coup, Varona was forced into exile. 
- He returned after Castro took over in 1959, but quickly grew alarmed by the communist shift and fled to Miami in 1960.
- Bender favored Varona because of his deep political roots and anti-communist stance, with Verona later becoming coordinator general of the Frente.
- His own son, Carlos de Varona, was a member of Brigade 2506 and fought at the Bay of Pigs
## Movimiento de Recuperación Revolucionaria (MRP) 
Founded in late 1959 by anti-Batista revolutionaries who quickly grew disillusioned with Castro's communist shift. Unique because it maintained a massive, active underground infiltration network inside Cuba alongside its exile operations in Miami.
### Manuel Ray Rivero
- Cuban engineer and the founder and leader of the MRP
- Originally a staunch anti-Batista revolutionary who served as Castro’s Minister of Public Works after the revolution but resigned in late 1959 due to Castro's communist shift.
- Fled to the U.S. in late 1960
- Advocated for "Fidelismo sin Fidel" (Castroism without Fidel, i.e. social reforms of the Cuban Revolution without communism) and believed that Castro should be overthrown from the inside by supporting the domestic underground rather than via a CIA-led invasion. 
- Distrusted by conservative Cuban exiles and CIA since he was politically left-of-center. Since Bender favored more right-leaning conservatives, he  systematically sidelined Manuel Ray and starved the MRP’s internal underground network of weapons, boats, and cash, choosing instead to funnel nearly all resources into the external amphibious force.



# Background
- Following Castro’s overthrow of Fulgencio Batista’s dictatorship in Cuba (January 1959), Cuba-US relations began to deteriorate.
- Castro's government confiscated private property (much of it owned by North American interests), sent agents to initiate revolutions in several Latin American countries, and established diplomatic and economic ties with leading socialist powers.
- By June 1959 Congress passed legislation enabling Eisenhower to take retaliatory steps
	- US cut off sugar purchases from Cuba and soon thereafter placed an embargo on all exports to Cuba except food and medicine.
- In May 1960, the CIA began planning an invasion of Cuba
- Right before Kennedy took over, Eisenhower in January 1961 broke diplomatic ties with Cuba

# Plan
## Domestic Uprising
- Joseph Newman was an acclaimed Latin American correspondent for the New York Herald Tribune. In the spring of 1961, Newman returned to the United States after a deep-dive reporting trip inside Cuba. Newman reported that opposition to Castro was confined to a "sizable minority" of the population and that the majority of Cubans still supported the revolutionary government and that Castro’s grip on power was remarkably secure. Newman warned that even anti-Castro Cubans living on the island would fiercely reject an invasion if they perceived it to be backed by the US because their past support for Batista and a U.S.-sponsored military intervention would actually cause a rally round the flag effect around Castro. According to historical records, Kennedy read Newman's pieces and quickly realized they contradicted the CIA’s optimistic projections.
# Invasion
- When: April 17, 1961
- Where: Bahía de Cochinos (Bay of Pigs), or Playa Girón (Girón Beach) to Cubans
![[bay_of_pigs.png]]
- Who: some 1,500 Cuban exiles opposed to Fidel Castro
- How: invasion was financed and directed by the U.S. government
### Details
- April 15: three U.S.-made airplanes piloted by Cubans bombed Cuban air bases
- April 17: US trained Cubans and using U.S. equipment landed at several sites (principal landing at the Bay of Pigs)
- April 19: invasion force loses final stronghold and more than 1,100 total men captured
### Aftermath
- Critics aid the CIA with supplying faulty information to Kennedy
- Following the failure, Carrillo and Cardona furiously condemned the CIA for mismanaging the operation, ignoring internal Cuban resistance networks, and keeping the Cuban leadership under virtual house arrest.
- In spite of Kennedy’s orders, supporters of Batista were included in the invasion force, whereas members of the noncommunist People’s Revolutionary Movement (most capable anti-Castro group) were excluded.
- From May 1961, Kennedy unofficially backed attempts to ransom the prisoners, but the efforts of the Tractors for Freedom Committee, headed by Eleanor Roosevelt, failed to raise the $28,000,000 needed for heavy-construction equipment demanded by Castro as reparations.
- Castro eventually released the prisoners  in exchange for $53,000,000 worth of food and medicine following negotiations with James B. Donovan.
- Between December 1962 - July 1965 the survivors were returned.
- Incident was crucial to the development of the Cuban missile crisis (October 1962)
