# Overview
- Kennedy escalated America's involvement in Vietnam due to his commitment to combat the spread of communism.
- He sent not just supplies and financial assistance, as Eisenhower had, but also 15,000 military advisers.


# Background
- Communist USSR + China used subversion and guerrilla warfare to spread communism during the Cold War and Kennedy and some of his close advisers believed that Vietnam presented an opportunity to test the United States’ ability to conduct a "counterinsurgency" against this. Kennedy himself called Vietnam, “the cornerstone of the free world in Southeast Asia” and would show U.S. determination to meet the challenge of communist expansion.
- **Domino theory**: Kennedy accepted domino theory without serious question
- Vietnam was important to Kennedy but other things were more important: 
	- Berlin Wall construction (August 1961)
	- conflict in Laos (1959-75)
	- Bay of Pigs failure (April 1961): made a success in Vietnam that much more important
- **Viet Cong Growth**: in 1960 U.S. intelligence estimated that ~4,000 Viet Cong infiltrations happened in the South; by 1962 the number was 12,900.
	- **Ho Chi Minh Trail**: Most of the firearms for PLAF/NLF soldiers actually came from the United States: large quantities of American rifles, carbines, machine guns, and mortars were captured from Saigon’s armed forces or simply sold to the Viet Cong by Diem’s corrupt officers and functionaries.
- **Diem Regime**: many of the South’s problems could be attributed to the incompetence and corruption of the Diem regime, but the South Vietnamese president had few American critics in Saigon or Washington. Instead, the U.S. administration made great efforts to reassure Diem of its support, dispatching Vice Pres. Lyndon B. Johnson to Saigon in May 1961 and boosting economic and military aid.


# Expanded Role
- **Rostow-Taylor report**: As the situation continued to deteriorate, Kennedy sent two key advisers, economist Walt W. Rostow and former army chief of staff Maxwell Taylor, to Vietnam in the fall of 1961 to assess conditions. 
	- They concluded that the South Vietnamese government was losing the war with the Viet Cong and had neither the will nor the ability to turn the tide on its own. 
	- Recommended a greatly expanded program of military assistance, including helicopters and armored personnel carriers, and an ambitious plan to place American advisers and technical experts at all levels and in all agencies of the Vietnamese government and military. 
	- Also recommended the introduction of a limited number of U.S. combat troops, a measure the Joint Chiefs of Staff had been urging as well.
- **Middle Approach**: Well aware of the domestic political consequences of “losing” another country to the communists, Kennedy could see no viable exit from Vietnam, but he also was reluctant to commit combat troops to a war in Southeast Asia. Instead, the administration proceeded with vigour and enthusiasm to carry out the expansive program of aid and guidance proposed in the Rostow-Taylor report. A ,
- **U.S. Military Assistance Command Vietnam (USMACV)**: new four-star general’s position (commander) was established in Saigon to guide the military assistance effort. 
- **US Military Presence Explodes**: U.S. military personnel in Vietnam, less than 800 in 1950s, rose to ~9,000 by the mid-1962.
