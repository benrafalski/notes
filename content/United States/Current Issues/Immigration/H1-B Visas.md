## How it Works
### Requirements
- **Specialty Occupation**: The job must require theoretical or practical application of highly specialized knowledge (e.g., engineering, IT, science, healthcare).
- Education: The worker must hold a U.S. bachelor's or higher degree (or foreign **equivalent**) in a related field.
- **Employer Sponsorship**: An individual cannot apply on their own; a U.S. employer must sponsor and file the petition.
- **Prevailing Wage**: The employer must pay the worker at least the prevailing wage or actual wage for that role in the geographic area (though this is hard to enforce in practice)
### Annual Process & Timeline
- **Registration (March)**: Employers register prospective workers in the USCIS electronic system.
- **Lottery System**: annual new visas are capped at 85,000 (65,000 for bachelors and 20,000 master's or higher degree holders) but there is much higher demand so there is a random selection lottery to chose who can apply.
- **Petition Filing (April–June)**: Selected registrants have their employers submit a certified Labor Condition Application (LCA) from the Department of Labor (DOL) along with Form I-129 to USCIS.
- **Work Start Date**: Approved petitions typically allow employment to begin on October 1 of that year.
### Duration
- **Time Limit**: Initially granted for up to 3 years and extendable for another 3 years, for a maximum total of 6 years.
- **Dual Intent**: meaning workers can legally hold the intention to transition to permanent residency (green card) via employer sponsorship while on H-1B status.
- **Job Mobility**: The visa is tied to the employer, though an H-1B holder can switch to a new U.S. employer as soon as the new company files a transfer petition.
